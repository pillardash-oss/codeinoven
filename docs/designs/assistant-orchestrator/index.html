CodeInOven / the assistant room
Design study · not implemented

The assistant page is a room, not a list.

The page you land on should feel like walking into a place where your staff is already standing: the orchestrator at the centre, every assistant around it at the hour it runs, and everything that needs you visible without opening anything. Talking to it is not a chat window, it is a despatch spine: your words arrive on the line, and every hand-off leaves it on its own elbow toward the assistant that took it.

The House

Seven assistants sit on a 24-hour face. A ring position is a time, so Release Notes at 17:00 really is standing where 17:00 is, and the gold hand is now. Marks carry the app's own state colours: blue running, amber waiting on you, red blocked.

The Despatch

One rule runs down the session. Who spoke is in the gutter, each dispatch branches off toward its owner with the reason it went there, and a blocked assistant shows up as a blocked line instead of a silent failure.

The Bench

Open any assistant and the room becomes its workbench, with the house still one click behind. The task that arrived from the orchestrator wears its provenance at the top and hands back to Chief from the header.

What it keeps

The app's tokens, its two bundled faces, hairline rules, sharp corners and gold used for structure rather than glow. No gradient wash, no glass, no purple.

What it changes

The assistant page stops being a directory of rows. The orchestrator becomes a place you stand in, and every assistant is a body in that room instead of an item in a list.

The open question

Whether a dispatched task reports into the despatch automatically, or only when it has something you must decide. These screens copy the first reading.
