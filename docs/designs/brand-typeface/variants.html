CodeInOven typeface · two cuts of one family

Oven Mono and Oven Box

One binary skeleton, drawn twice. Oven Mono squares the dots and the quote marks and leaves every letterform as comfortable as JetBrains Mono drew it. Oven Box takes the same face and turns out every corner the alphabet draws, so a bowl is a rectangle, a shoulder is a right angle, and the only round shape left in the cut is the parenthesis. Both land on the same 600 unit advance, so anything set in either occupies the same box.

The corner rule, and its one exception

A rounded corner in a TrueType outline is a quadratic whose control point already sits where that corner's two tangents cross. Turning the corner out to its mitre is therefore not a redraw: the control point becomes an on-curve point. A segment that turns less than twenty degrees was never a corner, so it is straightened instead, which is what takes the last bulge out of a bowl's side.

A drawn corner

The control point of a corner quadratic becomes the corner itself. Mitred, not bevelled.

04

The weight, measured against JetBrains Mono

The weight was not raised for the box cut. Both cuts instance JetBrains Mono rather than redraw it, so a stroke is the same outline at the same coordinate, and verify-family.py asserts that point for point on every glyph with no corner at all. What does move is the total ink, because a squared counter is a rectangle where the round one was a bowl. Ink is the filled area of a fixed 44 glyph sample, in em units of 1000, per measure-weight.py.

Oven Mono carries 300 to 800 and its light end is raised on purpose: weight 200 is a hairline on a dark plate, and the squared dots read better with a little more ink under them. Oven Box carries 200 to 800, matching JetBrains Mono to its own light end, since a squared corner spends a little more ink than a round one at the same stroke. If the two cuts should share one range, the honest fix is to take Oven Mono back to 200 as well, not to hold the box cut heavier than the reference.
