CodeInOven typeface · v1.000

Oven Mono

A real font file, not a drawing. It is built on the skeleton everyone already reads all day, JetBrains Mono, and then finished the way the mark is built: every dot becomes a square cut, every ASCII quote becomes a flat bar, and the light end stops being a hairline. Nothing else moves, so no line of the app reflows when it lands. This is the base cut; Oven Box is the same family with every drawn corner turned out to its mitre.

02

The one device: the dot is the knob

The mark is a squared vessel with a gold knob, and its shelf is a bar cut flat. So the face squares its dots. A square reads heavier than a circle of the same width, so each one is cut to 0.86 of its circle to hold the same weight of ink. The left glyph in each cell is what ships today.

U+002E full stop
..

A statement ends on a corner, the way the mark's shelf ends.

U+003A colon
::

Two cut squares. The pair still stacks exactly as before.

U+0021 exclamation
!!

The stem is untouched. Only the point under it is squared.

U+0027 apostrophe
''

The taper becomes a bar of constant width, cut flat at both ends.

03

Every weight, and none of them a hairline

The app ships at weight 200 today, which is a hairline on a dark plate and the first thing that strains the eye in a long session. The family starts at 300 and gives the interface a slightly heavier default, with 800 at the top for headings. The squared cut carries 200 to 800 instead, because a corner turned out to its mitre spends a little more ink than a round one: two cuts, sampled.

Against what ships today, at the same size

Both faces are drawn on a 1000 unit em with a 600 advance, so a line occupies the same box in either. The cut in play wears the same letterforms by construction, so the difference you are meant to see is the finish, not the alphabet: the marks in Oven Mono, the marks and the corners in Oven Box.

06

Why this is the answer, and what it costs

01 It is comfortable because it is not new. The skeleton is JetBrains Mono, which this app already sets everything in. A face invented from scratch has to earn four hours of reading before it earns a brand; this one inherits that and changes only the marks the brand actually owns.
02 It renders everywhere, from one file. Oven Mono is a variable TrueType font: the same binary installs in the editor, the terminal and the OS, and a 55 KB Latin woff2 covers the app. Fixed 600 advance, so nothing reflows.
03 The light end is legible. Oven Mono starts at 300 instead of 200, which removes the hairline that makes a dark plate fatiguing, and Oven Box carries 200 to 800 because its squared corners spend a little more ink at the same stroke. Either way the weight you pick is a comfort decision, not a brand one.
04 What it concedes. It is a derivative of an open font, not a drawn original, so the brand lives in the punctuation and the weight ramp rather than in the alphabet. If you want a face whose letterforms are yours alone, that is a multi-month drawing project and this is not it.
