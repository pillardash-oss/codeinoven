A solid disc, and what the scale costs

You dropped a reference: a solid blue disc with a bold mark knocked out of it. I cannot see images, so I measured it instead of guessing. It is one solid disc 260 pixels across with a single knockout whose widest span is 108 pixels, so the knockout takes 41 percent of the disc, there is no outline anywhere, and the disc supplies all the mass. That construction is the answer to the whole size conversation, because mass reads at 16px where an outline does not. Four candidates below draw our own meanings in it, at a size reconciled against the rest of the set.

2 · What the scale costs

ink mass, measured at the reference size

A disc is a heavier construction than an outline, so the first version was wrong and the measurement caught it. At full extent a disc measures 48 to 57 percent ink. The stroke set runs 17 to 44, and the assistant mark, the icon you told me not to change, is 24.9. A full disc would have out-shouted the very mark it sits beside. So the disc was shrunk to r=10.2, which measures 43.9 percent: the set's own ceiling, where Utilities sits at 42 and Tasks at 44.

Disc, reconciled solid 36 to 44% 20.5 Ships. At the set's ceiling, and still more visible than any stroke glyph because mass reads before outline does.

3 · Four browsers, knocked out of a disc

16px as the rail draws, 64px to read the drawing, and beside the assistant
The globe 38% ink

Heaviest of the four at 44 percent in the rose's company, 38 here. The equatorial band and the meridian are both cut through, so it reads as a sphere rather than a target. This is the safest of the four and the least distinctive.

The compass rose 44% ink

The strongest silhouette of the four. A four-point star cut out of the disc: the widest knockout, the clearest read at 16px, and the only one that is unmistakably not an icon anybody else uses for a browser. The risk is that a rose is chart vocabulary before it is web vocabulary.

The orbit 38% ink

The closest in spirit to your reference. A bold ring cut out of the disc, closer to the gesture you dropped than anything else here. A tilted ring was tried first and fragmented into specks at 16px, because a tilted ellipse's extremes are near-parallel edges the rasterizer splits. Upright, the thickness carries it.

The page 36% ink

The most literal. A page with a tab standing above it, cut out of the disc. It names exactly what the view is and it is the lightest of the four at 36 percent, so it sits closest to the assistant mark's 25. The cost is that a page shape is generic, and the tab reads as a chimney at the smallest size.

The honest trade. A disc is more visible than an outline at 16px, which is exactly what you asked for, and it costs extent: 20.5 units against the stroke set's 22.6. Mass reads before size does, so it still wins at the rail size, but if a disc browser is chosen while the other ten stay outlines, the rail has two visual languages in it and the browser will be the loudest item. The alternative is the whole rail in the disc construction, which is a bigger piece of work and would mean re-deciding every glyph.
