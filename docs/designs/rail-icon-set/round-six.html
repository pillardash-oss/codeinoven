CodeInOven · brand work · round six

The rose, and both browsers

Both corrections are in. The rose is the chosen browser mark, and the orbit is fixed: it was drawn with the ring inside the planet, and it is now a planet with a ring around it, which meant rebuilding it topologically rather than nudging the geometry. Below, both marks in the two places they will actually live: the global browser's chrome, and the thread browser's right dock and tab strip, which is where the rail's Browser view puts a thread's own pages.

CodeInOven finally, a rose that is not a chart

1 · The orbit, corrected

you were right, and it was worse than a flip

You said the ring is supposed to be around the planet, not inside it. Correct, and the cause was topological rather than geometric, so adjusting the radii would never have fixed it. The first version listed the planet's circle twice, and under a fill rule the ring's band therefore resolved as a hole inside the disc. A flat single-colour mark can only express planet, gap and ring if the three are nested outward from the centre, so a ray from the middle now crosses three boundaries and alternates ink, hole, ink, hole.

Before, rejected

Ring cut out of the inside of the disc, which reads as a ring lying inside the planet.

After, corrected

A solid planet with a gap around it and a ring beyond the gap.

What the nesting costs. All three bands have to clear 1.5 units, which is one device pixel at 16px, and three bands plus a planet have to fit in a 24-unit grid. So the planet is deliberately small. A very flat ring cannot encircle the planet at all, which is why the corrected orbit is rounder than the version you saw.

2 · The global browser

the tab strip and the address bar, with the rose as the fallback mark

The global browser draws a page's own favicon when it has one and falls back to a generic mark when it does not. That fallback is where the rose goes. This is the surface where the mark is seen most often, and at 12px in the tab strip, which is smaller than the rail.

Rose, in the global browser fallback glyph at 12px, tab strip and address bar
New tab github.com/pillardash-oss the old globe, for comparison
codeinoven.com

Read it at the size it ships. The first two tabs above draw the rose at 12px, the size the tab strip uses, which is smaller than the rail's 16px. At that size a rose's four points are the whole mark, and the inner details are gone. That is fine, because the point of the cut is the silhouette, but it is worth seeing before it ships rather than after.

3 · The thread browser

the right dock and the tab strip a thread's own pages use

A thread's browser is a panel on the conversation, not the whole window, so its mark appears in two places: the right context dock, where the panel is toggled, and the panel's own tab strip. Today both draw a Lucide globe-with-code (`GlobeCode`), which is the closest thing the set has to the global browser's mark and therefore the least able to tell the two apart.

Right context dock 16px, the rail's own size

The question this raises. If the global browser wears the rose and the thread browser wears the orbit, the two are told apart by shape, which is exactly what the current pair of globes fails to do. The alternative is the rose in both, on the grounds that a browser is a browser and only the context differs. I have shown the split here because it is the version that uses both corrected marks; say the word and I will make it consistent instead.

Where the orbit is weaker. At 12px the orbit's gap closes and it reads as a plain disc, which the rose never does. So if the orbit is used in a tab strip rather than the dock, it is the rose that should go there. That is a real difference between the two, not a preference.

5 · Still open

the two questions this round adds, and the one it does not close
  • Split or consistent. Rose for the global browser and orbit for the thread browser, or the rose in both. The split uses both corrected marks and distinguishes the two at a glance; consistency is simpler and says a browser is a browser.
  • Scope. The rose is currently the rail's Browser view. The tab strips and the dock are three more call sites, and the thread browser's four `GlobeCode` uses are the ones that would change.
  • The update button is still untouched since round three: one control cycling six state glyphs, waiting on what that control is rather than how it is drawn.
