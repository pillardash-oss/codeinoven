CodeInOven · brand work

The rail icon set, two directions

Every icon the app draws today comes from Lucide. This is the first surface to get its own language: the left view rail, eleven glyphs in two complete passes. Both passes are drawn on the same rules the rest of the app already keeps: right angles only, square caps, miter joins, one ink colour that follows the theme, and an ember that appears on the same terms the logo's does.

2 · Direction A · The Oven Wire

outline tier · 2px ink · mitered · 24 grid

Hairline geometry on the rail's existing 24 grid, drawn entirely in right angles: a rectangular frame is the oven, a rule with entries is a thread, a frame with a mark in it is a scope. Square caps and miter joins, so every corner meets at a point the way the logo's brackets do. A curve appears only where the curve is the object itself. The gold pixel is the logo's ember, used once per glyph at most, and only where the mark would otherwise be ambiguous.

3 · Direction B · The Slab

solid tier · ink mass, meaning knocked out

The outline is gone. Each glyph is a solid block of ink whose meaning is cut out of it, exactly how the tray mark is built: a filled slab with a single knockout. Geometry widens to an 18 grid so the counters stay open at 16px, and the fill, not the stroke, carries the weight. These read as stamps or stencils, and they hold at a glance in a way hairlines never do at the left edge of a window.

4 · Given to scale

a size test rather than a beauty shot

The rail draws at 16px and nothing else. These are the three heaviest glyphs of each pass at 12, 16, 20 and 28px, plus the assistant mark the rail already ships, so the two passes are weighed against the one mark that is already settled.

What changes either way. The rail stops drawing Lucide. Nothing else in the app moves yet: this study is one surface and eleven meanings, so a direction can be settled on a small, reversible piece of work before the other roughly three hundred glyphs are drawn. The assistant mark is the exception and stays as it is; it is the anchor both passes answer to, and replacing it is a separate decision.

5 · The trade

what each one costs, stated plainly
A · The Oven Wire outline
  • Reads as an instrument. Thin, exact, technical. It sits beside a terminal-native app and a monospace UI without competing with either.
  • Scales down honestly. At 12px the containers survive as containers; only interior detail drops out.
  • Risk: a hairline glyph looks lighter than the Lucide icons beside it in every other panel, so the rail can read as unfinished while the rest of the app is still Lucide.
  • Risk: 2px ink at 16px is a 1.33px rendered stroke, which the display rounds unevenly at 1x. This is the tier's real weakness.
Best when the whole app moves to the outline tier later, so nothing sits beside an outline glyph.
B · The Slab solid
  • Reads as a stamp. Every glyph is one unmistakable shape, which is exactly what a 32px tool button needs at the edge of a busy window.
  • Carries the tray mark's DNA directly. The tray is a solid slab with a knockout, so the small-size language is already proven in this product.
  • Risk: heavier than every other icon in the app, so the rail becomes the loudest column on screen until the rest catches up.
  • Risk: a solid grid of ink is easy to over-apply; the ember is a hole rather than a dot, which is a different treatment from the logo's.
Best if the rail should lead the identity and the rest of the app follows it rather than the other way round.
