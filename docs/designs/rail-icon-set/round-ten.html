Every rail: what exists now, beside the CIO rebuild

Each rail is drawn twice in the same order. The left strip is the Lucide glyph the app draws today, at the exact stroke that surface uses. The right strip is the CIO slot for the same place. The table under each pair gives the number, both drawings at 28px, and the two strokes, so a slot can be answered by number.

The two browser slots are the ones that trip people up. The app draws Lucide's globe and globe-with-code there today, and will draw the CIO planet and bubble instead, so those pairs differ as much as any on the page.

5 · The standard the right column is built to

  • Box, 22.6 units of ink. Lucide draws inside 22.0. Round four tested 22.0 to 23.1 and 22.6 is the largest that keeps solid ink off the outermost row.
  • Stroke, 1.9 on every glyph. Today the rail and the dock use 1.8 and the header and tabs use 2, so the same set is drawn at two weights. The CIO set is one weight.
  • Corners, 1.6 to 2 units, matching the assistant mark.
  • Floor, 1.5 units, because at 16px one unit under 1.5 stops surviving the rasterizer.
  • Fit, every glyph fills the box, scaled about its own ink centre. The two chevrons are the exception: a direction is not a picture, so they fit a 16-unit indicator box instead of the 22.6 picture box.
  • Drawn by hand. Nothing in the right column is Lucide geometry. Nine glyphs are pass F's own drawings carried over; the other twenty-one were drawn for this round in the same language: a rounded container, one or two solid anchors for mass, and detail spent only where it changes the meaning.

The left column is Lucide, captured from the source each surface imports. The right column is drawn by hand, so the two are genuinely different drawings and not the same shape at two sizes. The page fetches nothing: if a glyph were missing here it would be missing for a reason the build refuses to accept.
